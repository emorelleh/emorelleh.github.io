      <p>
        My teaching covers criminal law, criminology, criminal justice,
        environmental crime, and interdisciplinary approaches to crime,
        harm, security, and social response.
      </p>
    </header>

    <section id="teaching-approach">
      <h2>Teaching approach</h2>

      <p>
        I understand university teaching as a space in which students
        develop the ability to analyse legal and criminological problems
        critically, connect theoretical knowledge with evidence, and
        construct well-reasoned responses to complex social and
        environmental challenges.
      </p>

      <p>
        My teaching combines legal analysis, criminological theory,
        case-law study, empirical evidence, and discussion of real cases.
        I use active methodologies that encourage students to identify
        problems, compare different interpretations, defend arguments,
        and assess the practical consequences of legal and criminal-policy
        decisions.
      </p>

      <p>
        Interdisciplinary learning is particularly important in my courses.
        Questions involving crime and harm often require dialogue between
        criminal law, criminology, psychology, environmental science,
        marine ecology, public policy, and professional practice.
      </p>
    </section>

    <section id="specific-forms">
      <h2>Specific Forms of Criminality</h2>

      <p>
        <a href="https://ujiapps.uji.es/sia/rest/publicacion/2026/estudio/243">
          Specific Forms of Criminality
        </a>
        is a fourth-year compulsory course in the Bachelor’s Degree in
        Criminology and Security at Universitat Jaume I.
      </p>

      <p>
        The course examines forms of criminality that require specialised
        legal, criminological, and empirical analysis. Students work with
        academic literature, legislation, case law, criminological data,
        and practical problems in order to understand both the characteristics
        of these phenomena and the institutional responses developed to
        address them.
      </p>

      <p>
        The course combines structured theoretical materials with
        discussion-based seminars, practical cases, guided readings,
        and student participation. Particular attention is given to the
        critical evaluation of criminalisation processes, prevention
        strategies, criminal policy, and the social and ecological
        consequences of crime and harm.
      </p>
    </section>

    <section id="supervision">
      <h2>Supervision</h2>

      <p>
        I supervise bachelor’s dissertations, master’s dissertations,
        research projects, and external placements in law, criminology,
        criminal justice, and related interdisciplinary fields.
      </p>

      <p>
        My supervision interests include environmental crime, green and
        blue criminology, ecological justice, criminal law, criminal policy,
        restorative justice, corporate crime, emerging forms of criminality,
        security, victimisation, and the relationship between law and
        ecological evidence.
      </p>

      <p>
        I encourage students to formulate clear research questions,
        work critically with primary and secondary sources, select methods
        appropriate to their objectives, and present their conclusions
        with analytical rigour and academic integrity.
      </p>
    </section>

    <section id="clinical-learning">
      <h2>Clinical and experiential learning</h2>

      <p>
        I was the second Chair of
        <a href="https://clinilex.uji.es">
          Legal and Criminological Clinic at Universitat Jaume I
        </a>,
        from November 2023 to September 2026, where students connect academic learning with real legal,
        criminological, and social problems.
      </p>

      <p>
        Clinical learning allows students to participate in supervised
        projects, analyse real cases, collaborate with institutions and
        civil-society organisations, and develop professional competencies
        while reflecting on ethics, vulnerability, social justice, and
        public responsibility.
      </p>

      <p>
        This approach is also incorporated into activities developed
        through the Aula IbizaPreservation de Criminalidad Azul and other
        collaborations concerning marine environmental harm, ecological
        justice, public policy, and the protection of coastal communities.
      </p>
    </section>

    <section id="teaching-innovation">
      <h2>Teaching innovation</h2>

      <div class="teaching-project">
        <h3>ECO-CRIM-2023</h3>

        <p class="project-meta">
          Principal Investigator · Universitat Jaume I · 2024–2025
        </p>

        <p>
          This educational-innovation project applies integrated
          case-based learning to the study of economic and ecological
          criminality. It connects legal reasoning, criminological
          analysis, evidence assessment, and collaborative problem-solving.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="teaching-project">
        <h3>Case-based learning</h3>

        <p>
          My teaching uses complex cases and microcases to help students
          identify legally relevant facts, distinguish between competing
          interpretations, evaluate evidence, and construct reasoned
          legal and criminological responses.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="teaching-project">
        <h3>Seminar-based learning</h3>

        <p>
          Seminars are organised around guided questions, mandatory
          readings, structured discussion, and reasoned participation.
          This format encourages students to move beyond the reproduction
          of content and engage directly with academic debates and
          practical dilemmas.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="teaching-project">
        <h3>Teaching materials</h3>

        <p>
          I develop original learning materials in criminal law,
          criminology, environmental crime, and green criminology.
          These resources combine conceptual explanation with readings,
          practical cases, seminar activities, and tools for continuous
          assessment.
        </p>
      </div>
    </section>
